# 달에 미소를 보낸 여덟 살 아이 — 영어 학습 영상 원고

> 제작 메모: L4/B2. 영어와 한국어를 각각 적합한 음성으로 생성합니다. 대괄호는 화면·편집 지시이며 읽지 않습니다. 실제 음성 길이를 확인한 뒤 쉐도잉 시간을 조정합니다.

## 도입 · 한국어

안녕하세요. 오늘은 한 어린이의 작은 상상이 달 주위를 여행하게 된 이야기를 영어로 들어보겠습니다. 먼저 전체 이야기를 듣고, 내용을 함께 살펴본 뒤 표현과 섀도잉을 연습해 볼게요.

## 첫 듣기 · 영어

[영어 자막 없이 전체 이야기를 듣습니다.]

What could an eight-year-old send to the Moon? Not a rocket, and not a scientific experiment. In April 2026, a small smiling toy traveled around the Moon with four astronauts. Its journey began with a drawing by a California student named Lucas Ye.

NASA was preparing Artemis II, its first mission to carry astronauts near the Moon in more than fifty years. The agency invited people around the world to design a special passenger for the spacecraft. More than 2,600 ideas arrived from over fifty countries. Lucas entered with a character he called Rise.

Rise looked like a cheerful little Moon wearing a colorful cap. The cap represented Earth, while details on the toy pointed to earlier space missions. Lucas had drawn inspiration from Earthrise, a famous photograph taken during Apollo 8 in 1968. His design connected the history of exploration with a new journey.

The astronauts had an important reason to bring a soft toy aboard. Rise was a zero-gravity indicator, not just a decoration. During launch, the crew remained strapped into their seats. Once the spacecraft reached weightlessness, the toy could lift and float on its tether, giving everyone a simple visual sign of the change.

NASA specialists then had to turn a child's idea into an object safe enough for spaceflight. Specialists at the agency's Goddard Space Flight Center made the final version. They checked materials and construction because even a playful passenger must meet spacecraft safety requirements. Creativity had to work alongside careful engineering.

There was another surprise hidden inside Rise. A small memory card carried more than 5.6 million names submitted by people around the world. Lucas and his family also watched the launch from Florida on April 1, 2026. The tiny traveler represented far more than one young designer.

The astronauts flew around the Moon and returned safely to Earth on April 10. Their journey tested systems needed for future missions, while Rise offered a friendly reminder of the people following along at home. A child's drawing had become part of a remarkable moment in space history.

Lucas did not build the rocket or fly the spacecraft. His contribution was different: he imagined something that others could turn into reality. The story of Rise shows how a small idea can travel farther than anyone might expect, especially when curiosity meets teamwork and careful preparation.
